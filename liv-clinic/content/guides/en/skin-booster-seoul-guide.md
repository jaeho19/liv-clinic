Skin boosters are one of the treatments visitors most often ask about before a trip to Seoul: small injections aimed at skin quality — hydration, elasticity and glow — rather than at volume or muscle movement. This guide sets out what [LIV Plastic Surgery](/international) actually publishes about them: which products are on the price list, what a session costs, how long it takes, what your skin looks like afterwards, and how the recommended course of several sessions fits (or does not fit) into a short stay. LIV is a non-surgical anti-aging clinic in Gangnam, one minute from Sinsa Station and next to Garosu-gil. International patients pay from the same price list as Korean patients, and you can book directly in English.

## What a skin booster is, in the clinic's words

The clinic's [skin booster page](/antiaging/skinbooster) describes the treatment for the full face, the neck and the backs of the hands. It is suggested for people who are concerned about:

- dry skin and a lack of moisture
- reduced elasticity and fine lines
- a dull skin tone and skin that looks tired
- skin recovery after laser treatment

People often ask whether a skin booster is the same thing as a "water-glow injection". The clinic's answer is that the two are similar, but a skin booster contains a wider range of ingredients. Which product suits you is not something to settle from a list: the clinic chooses it with you at the consultation, based on your skin condition and your goals.

## Time, anesthesia and downtime at a glance

| Item | What the clinic states |
|---|---|
| Treatment time | 30–45 minutes |
| Anesthesia | Topical anesthetic cream |
| Downtime | 1–3 days (micro-needle marks) |
| When results show | Hydration right away; elasticity and glow after 2–4 weeks |
| Recommended course | 3–4 sessions at 2–4 week intervals |
| After the course | Maintenance every 2–3 months is recommended |
| Return visit for one session | Same day, no revisit required |

The downtime is the part to plan around. Small injection marks and some redness are possible after the session, and the clinic gives 1–3 days as the recovery period. If you have photos, a dinner or a meeting you care about, put the appointment a few days before or after it rather than on the same day.

## Aftercare the clinic asks for

- Avoid makeup and alcohol on the day of treatment.
- Be thorough with sun protection afterwards.
- Expect fine needle marks and possible redness for the first few days.

You receive a written aftercare guide in English, and after you return home the clinic can review photos you send by messenger or chat. If something does not look right, message the clinic rather than waiting.

## How many sessions, and what that means for a short trip

This is the honest point for visitors. The clinic recommends skin boosters as a basic course of 3–4 sessions at 2–4 week intervals, followed by maintenance every 2–3 months. A one-week holiday covers one session of that course, not all of it.

What the clinic says about a single session: you may notice an improvement in hydration after one session, but the optimal effect needs the full course. So a realistic plan looks like one of these:

- **One session on this trip.** Treat it as a first session. You get the same-day treatment and no return visit is required for that session.
- **A course across several visits.** If you come to Seoul regularly — for work, or every month or two — sessions can be spaced to match the 2–4 week interval. Tell the clinic your travel pattern when you first message.
- **A longer stay.** If you are in Korea for a month or more, the interval allows more than one session within the stay.

Which of these makes sense for you is decided at the consultation with the surgeon; the clinic may also suggest a different treatment if a single visit is all you have. For trip timing with other treatments, see the guide on [downtime and flight planning](/guides/downtime-flight-travel-plan).

A simple way to place one session in a short itinerary:

1. **Before you travel:** message the clinic with your concern, a few photos and your dates, and receive a price estimate.
2. **Early in the trip:** in-person consultation and, in most cases, treatment on the same day. Allow time for the anesthetic cream before the 30–45 minute treatment.
3. **The next 1–3 days:** injection marks and possible redness; keep plans flexible and stay careful with sun protection.
4. **After you fly home:** photo follow-up by messenger or chat.

## FAQ

### How much does a skin booster cost in Seoul?

At LIV the published prices start from KRW 190,000 per session (Rejuran HB plus 1 cc or Eye Rejuran 1 cc) and go up to KRW 600,000 (Rejuran HB plus 4 cc), depending on the product and the volume. Prices are per session, exclude VAT, and are the same for international and Korean patients. The exact price is confirmed after the consultation.

### Is a skin booster the same as a water-glow injection?

They are similar, but according to the clinic a skin booster contains a wider range of ingredients. The price list includes both skin booster products and a Glow Injection, and the choice between them is made at the consultation based on your skin condition and goals.

### How long is the downtime?

The clinic states 1–3 days, with micro-needle marks and possible redness. Avoid makeup and alcohol on the day of treatment and be thorough with sun protection afterwards.

### How many sessions do I need?

The recommended basic course is 3–4 sessions at 2–4 week intervals, then maintenance every 2–3 months. You may notice better hydration after one session, but the clinic says the optimal effect requires the full course.

### Can I have it done on the same day as my consultation?

In most cases, yes. A session takes 30–45 minutes with topical anesthetic cream, needs no hospitalization, and no return visit is required for that session.
